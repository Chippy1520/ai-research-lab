## What this means for Chathuka

Compute scale is becoming part of the humanoid stack, but the entry-level evidence still rewards engineers who can connect algorithms to real hardware. Figure’s announcement highlights training infrastructure; Apptronik’s role shows what must happen after training—diagnose whether failures come from calibration/geometry, data/model quality, simulation transfer, real-time software, or hardware bring-up. The internships reinforce firmware, power, test, controls, PCB, manufacturing, and repeatability.

### One evidence-linked portfolio/study action

Extend the existing **real-time robot-control and learning stack** with a reproducible **sim-to-hardware failure-attribution benchmark** rather than starting a disconnected project:

1. train or tune one manipulation/control policy under fixed seeds and log dataset/model versions;
2. inject calibration error, observation delay, sensor noise, actuator saturation, and model mismatch separately;
3. compare a learned policy against a model-based baseline, reporting success rate, latency percentiles, confidence intervals, and deadline misses;
4. add a fault tree that attributes failures to geometry/calibration, perception, policy/data, timing, or actuator limits;
5. expose the experiment through typed Python plus one deterministic C++ control/inference boundary, with tests and a one-command reproduction path.

This is supported by persistent multi-company evidence—controls in 81 roles, embedded/real-time in 72, simulation in 56, perception in 51, C++ in 48—and is specifically reinforced today by Apptronik’s end-to-end manipulation posting. Figure’s compute announcement makes experiment/data lineage more important, not less.
